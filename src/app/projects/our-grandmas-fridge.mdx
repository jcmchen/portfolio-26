Description of Missing Objects Library from Prof. Asma Kazmi's [**website**](https://www.asmakazmi.com/mol):

Missing Objects Library (MOL) is a curated, web-based repository of handmade 3D objects that are designed with an intersectional, feminist lens. MOL is displayed as both a physical video installation and a web-based, free model library. MOL offers an alternative to commercial, status quo storefronts that provide digital assets for game design and special effects. Objects sold in these spaces are typically devoid of provenance, and they continually reinscribe false notions of neutrality while privileging a white, cis, heteronormative dominance.

In contrast, MOL is an open platform with downloadable models that accurately represent the world we inhabit. MOL disrupts historical gatekeeping performed by “neutral” marketplaces by offering 3D modeled objects that span a wide range of identities, abilities, and affinities. In addition to critiquing existing 3D model storefronts, MOL builds community by offering an economic system of reciprocity, where technological representations of things are exchanged to produce meaningful relations and effects.

## Our Grandma's Fridge Curatorial Statement:

What first comes to mind when you are prompted to think of “Asian food”? Often, it conjures images of familiar dishes, popularized brands, and iconic meals that have become synonymous with Asian cuisine in the West. However, beneath these well-known representations lies a rich tapestry of culinary traditions that goes beyond the stereotypical.

Step into the heart of our exhibition, where "Grandma's Fridge" serves as a poignant tribute to the diverse narratives woven into the fabric of the Asian diaspora. As four individuals identifying with different sub-groups of Asia, we unravel unique stories and culinary traditions, spotlighting the often-overlooked aspects of Asian kitchens and the diverse flavors defining our collective heritage.

Despite its lack of temperature control and seemingly ordinary appearance, “Grandma’s Fridge” is no ordinary “fridge”. It safeguards not just kitchenware and foodstuff but protects against intrusion, hunger, and serves as a beacon of sustenance during times of hardship. The wear and tear, along with repairs, seen on its exterior, bear witness to a time when fixing and adapting were indispensable lifelines.

“Grandma's Fridge" becomes a crossroads of cultural intersections, where the familiar and the unfamiliar coexist, reflecting the vast and dynamic mosaic of Asian culinary heritage. Through this virtual exhibit, we invite you to savor the nuances of these culinary stories, appreciating the diversity that defines our collective Asian identity.

This ensemble acts as a conduit between the familiar and the unfamiliar, honoring the richness that characterizes Asian kitchens. These objects represent merely a fraction of what is absent from commercial markets, driven by a preference for mass-produced, easily recognizable items. “Grandma's Fridge” definitely challenges this narrative, putting authenticity and uniqueness in the spotlight.
